Automatic AI meeting minutes: the guide for industrial teams

Technical meetings, customer appointments, site meetings: AI writes your minutes automatically, with guaranteed European hosting.

A site meeting finishes at 5 pm. The team leader climbs back into his van and heads for the next appointment. The minutes? He will write them this evening, or tomorrow morning, or... never. Three weeks later, nobody remembers who was supposed to order the spare part, or why the deadline was pushed back.

Every industrial SME knows this scenario. Meeting minutes are the task everyone knows is useful and nobody ever does, for lack of time, for lack of energy at the end of the day. The result: decisions get lost, verbal commitments evaporate, and information only circulates among the two or three people who were in the room.

Minutes: the task everyone puts off

In an industrial SME, there is no shortage of meetings: site reviews with the customer, technical updates with the design office, sales appointments, the weekly management committee. Each one generates decisions, figures, commitments on lead times. And each one should, in theory, result in written minutes.

In practice, that is rarely the case. The manager who comes out of the meeting moves straight on to something else. The minutes, if they exist at all, are written from memory several days later, with all the approximations that implies. Or they rely on handwritten notes taken during the discussion — which forces you to choose between listening and writing, rarely managing both at once.

The cost of having no record is rarely visible straight away. It is paid later: a disagreement over what was approved in the meeting, a lost piece of technical information, a new colleague who cannot find the history of a file.

How AI turns a meeting into usable minutes

The mechanics have become simple. The user records the meeting — on a phone, a computer, or by connecting the tool to a video-conferencing service. Three steps then run automatically.

Transcription

The AI transcribes the entire discussion into text. No more reminders scribbled while the customer is talking: your attention stays on the conversation.

Diarisation: who said what

This is the least-known step and the most useful one in meetings with several speakers. The AI identifies the different speakers and attributes each contribution to the right person. In a site review with the customer, the subcontractor and the project manager, that changes everything: you know exactly who committed to what.

Structuring

Raw text is not minutes. The AI reformats it into a usable document: context, points discussed, decisions taken, actions to be carried out and the people responsible. It is this document that is emailed to the team, not the full transcript.

Minutes that never get written are worth nothing to anyone — even if they could have changed everything.

Confidentiality: why European hosting matters in practice

A meeting in an industrial company is not trivial. People discuss prices negotiated with a customer, manufacturing methods that make up the company's value, sometimes sensitive HR matters in the management committee. Recording and transcribing these discussions with an AI tool means entrusting that content to a third party.

Most automatic minutes solutions on the market send recordings to American servers, often with the same cloud giants, whatever the nationality of the publisher on the label. Once the data has left, the SME no longer has any real visibility or control over where it goes and how it is processed.

This is not a matter of abstract compliance. It is a very concrete question: should a recording in which your workshop manager details a manufacturing process, or in which your customer mentions his target price, pass through infrastructure subject to foreign law? For an SME that has built its value on industrial know-how, the answer is no.

Practical use cases in industry

Site meeting

Customer, subcontractor, project manager: three voices, often with dense technical vocabulary. Diarisation makes it possible to find exactly who approved which point, and structured minutes avoid misunderstandings about lead times or reservations raised.

Technical project review

The design office and production discuss a drawing change or a choice of process. Automatic minutes keep a record of the technical decisions — useful three months later when the question comes back on the table.

Sales appointment

A salesperson who records the appointment can concentrate fully on the conversation, without juggling a notebook. The generated minutes capture the needs expressed, the budget mentioned, the objections — material the salesperson usually forgets to note down afterwards.

Management committee

Strategic decisions, HR matters, budget trade-offs: an SME management committee benefits from leaving a reliable record, without depending on the memory of a single participant taking notes while also chairing the meeting.
