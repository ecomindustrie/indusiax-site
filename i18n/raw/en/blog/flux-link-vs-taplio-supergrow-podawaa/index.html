Flux-Link vs Taplio, Supergrow and Podawaa: which tool lets you post on LinkedIn without writing?

Taplio, Supergrow, Podawaa: which AI tool should you choose to post on LinkedIn without writing? An honest comparison, with real prices and risks.

Indusiax20 July 20268 min read

Posting on LinkedIn without writing a single line: several tools promise it, but not in the same way. Taplio and Supergrow generate text from an idea or a guided conversation. Podawaa generates almost nothing — it amplifies what already exists. Flux-Link does something else: it turns three dictated sentences and a few job-site photos into a published post, with no credit tiers and no settings to learn.

The right choice depends on who you are. A consultant building a personal brand does not have the same needs as the managing director of an industrial SME who simply wants to show what the company does in the field, once a week, without losing an evening to it.

This comparison is based on the prices and features published by Taplio, Supergrow and Podawaa, checked in July 2026. The aim: to see clearly what each one does well — and what does not fit an industrial SME.

Taplio, Supergrow, Podawaa: three different approaches

Before comparing, we need to draw a distinction. Taplio and Supergrow are genuine competitors in AI content generation: both turn an idea or a voice into text ready to publish. Podawaa belongs to a different family of tools: it is first and foremost an amplification platform — engagement pods in which members of a network automatically like and comment on each other's posts. AI writing is only a secondary feature there, limited to hooks. Comparing it point by point with Flux-Link, Taplio or Supergrow on writing would make no sense, so we deal with it separately.

It still has its place in this comparison because many industrial managers confuse "posting" with "being visible". For an industrial company, being visible on LinkedIn starts with showing real work — not with generating artificial engagement around nothing, as we explain in our article on LinkedIn for industrial companies.

Taplio: solid AI, but a misleading entry price

Taplio is a French company, founded in Paris in 2022 and acquired the same year by Lempire, the publisher of lemlist. It is one of the most widely used tools on the market for personal branding and B2B prospecting, with two recognised strengths: detailed analytics and a database of more than 200,000 benchmarked posts, which let you compare your performance with that of other creators, and a free Chrome extension that users like for analysing profiles.

The problem lies elsewhere: the price. The Starter plan at $39/month gives access to scheduling and basic analytics — but no AI credits. To actually generate posts with AI, you have to move up to the Standard/Growth plan at $65-69/month, which includes 250 AI credits. The headline price is therefore not the one that lets you do what you came to the tool for. Another point to be aware of: Taplio authenticates via cookies rather than LinkedIn's official API, a method that earned several users account warnings during a detection wave in April 2025. On content, the criticism that comes up most often on Reddit, Product Hunt and Trustpilot is that the generated posts sound "generic", like a recycled template — and that AI credits run out quickly for anyone who posts regularly.

Supergrow: good voice personalisation, but a non-European tool

Supergrow banks on a real strength: personalisation. Its "Content DNA" feature learns the user's writing style, and "PostCast" takes the idea further — an AI-guided conversation of 15 to 20 minutes, then turned into 3 to 7 posts. Of the three competitors examined here, it is the mechanism closest to dictation.

But two limitations weigh on it. First, the price: the Starter plan at $19/month gives access to a single profile without analytics, and you need the Pro plan at $39/month for unlimited generation — with only 2 PostCast sessions at entry level. Second, and this may be the most important point for a French SME: Supergrow is published by a company based in Bangalore, India, with no precise data hosting location found to date — a GDPR compliance point to check yourself before connecting a business account to it. Users also point to analytics they consider superficial, adding little to what LinkedIn already shows for free, and visual tools still seen as immature.

Podawaa: amplification rather than writing — and a real risk to be aware of

Podawaa is a French company, founded in Montpellier by the creators of Waalaxy, a benchmark in this market. Its "Community" mode runs a system of reciprocal automatic likes and comments via a Chrome extension; its "Private" mode, 100% cloud-based with no extension, is presented as safer. That is a real advantage for a company looking for a French tool.

But Podawaa does not solve the initial problem: producing content. Its AI feature is limited to basic hooks; most of the text is still yours to write. And Community mode carries a documented risk: engagement automation breaches LinkedIn's terms of use, and several accounts report restrictions on the profiles of people using this type of tool. The engagement generated is also judged to be of low quality, because it comes from members outside the real target audience — not customers or prospects, just other users of the same pod.

Posting on LinkedIn without writing a line, without risk and without settings: that is how Flux-Link works.

What Flux-Link does differently

Compared with these three tools, Flux-Link is not playing the same game. It was not designed to build a personal brand or to generate leads: it was designed for a managing director or a technician who has just left a job site, has three things to say, and has neither the time nor the inclination to learn yet another piece of software.

The method fits in one sentence: you dictate three sentences, add photos, and the AI writes and publishes. No AI credits to keep an eye on as with Taplio, no carousels or analytics dashboards to configure as with Supergrow, no extension to install or "community" mode to figure out as with Podawaa.

The best tool is not the one with the most features. It is the one you actually use, on a Friday evening, between two jobs.

The other difference lies in what Flux-Link talks about. Taplio and Supergrow are built for general personal branding or B2B prospecting; neither has a recognised focus on technical or industrial content. Flux-Link, by contrast, was built around the field — job sites, machines, interventions — because it was born in an industrial engineering SME, like the rest of the Indusiax Suite (see also our comparison of software for industrial SMEs).

Finally, the price: €12 per user per month, a single tier, shown on the pricing page — with no teaser version that hides the useful features behind a more expensive plan, as is the case with Taplio. Find out more on the Flux-Link page.

Frequently asked questions

Is Flux-Link really simpler than Taplio?

Yes, in the way it is used. Taplio asks you to write a starting idea, pick a format and manage AI credits. Flux-Link asks you to dictate three sentences and add photos — the AI does the rest, publishing included, with no counter to keep an eye on.

Could Flux-Link get you penalised by LinkedIn, as Podawaa can?

No, the approach is different. Podawaa automates likes and comments between accounts, which breaches LinkedIn's terms of use. Flux-Link simply publishes a post written from what you dictated — no engagement automation.

What does each tool really cost?

Flux-Link: €12/user/month, a single tier. Taplio: $39/month without AI, $65-69/month for AI writing. Supergrow: $19/month with limits, $39/month for unlimited generation. Podawaa: around €19-30/month entry-level, up to €49-99/month depending on options.

Is Flux-Link suitable for technical industrial content?

That is what it was built for. Unlike Taplio and Supergrow, designed for general personal branding, Flux-Link was designed to turn site photos and a few dictated sentences into a LinkedIn post anyone can follow — without the user having to simplify the technical content themselves.

No tool is better than the others in absolute terms — there is a tool suited to each use. Taplio remains solid for anyone doing B2B prospecting who wants in-depth analytics. Supergrow personalises the voice well for anyone building a personal brand. Podawaa may have its place for engagement, provided you accept its documented risk. For a managing director or technician at an industrial SME who simply wants to show their work without spending time on it, Flux-Link remains the most direct of the four.
