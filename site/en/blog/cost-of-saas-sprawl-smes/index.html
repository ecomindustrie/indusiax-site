The real cost of software sprawl for an industrial SME

Why stacking a CRM, Excel, WhatsApp and video calls costs more than it seems — and what a connected suite really changes.

Indusiax20 July 20267 min read

A general-purpose CRM signed up for by a former sales rep, an Excel file shared on a drive that nobody updates any more, WhatsApp to reach technicians in the field, a separate video tool for meetings, and LinkedIn that nobody really manages. If this list sounds familiar, you are not alone — it is daily life for most French industrial SMEs.

The straight answer: the real cost of software sprawl does not show up on your bank statements. It shows up in the time lost passing information around by hand, in the mistakes that stem from something being forgotten, and in the fact that no tool knows what the others have seen.

This article sets out those hidden costs, then makes an honest calculation — without exaggeration — between keeping separate tools and moving to a connected suite.

How software sprawl sets in, without anyone deciding it

Nobody wakes up one morning thinking "I'm going to stack up five tools that don't talk to each other". Sprawl builds up through small decisions, each one reasonable at the time.

A sales rep joins and signs up for a general-purpose CRM because it is what they used at their previous employer. They leave two years later; the subscription keeps running, half filled in. In the meantime, someone has created a "customer tracking" Excel file because it was simpler for everyone. The field technicians, for their part, never had access to the CRM — too cumbersome, not designed for them — so they communicate with the workshop by text message or WhatsApp. For internal meetings, people got used to a generic video tool, unconnected to anything else. And LinkedIn, which is supposed to raise the company's profile, is handled "when we have time" — in other words, rarely.

Each building block, taken in isolation, has a modest cost and a logical justification. It is the stacking that causes the problem — not any individual tool.

Hidden cost no. 1: data that never talks to each other

This is the most concrete and most expensive point. The sales rep negotiates a contract without knowing that the technician visited the same customer last week for a complicated repair. The technician, for their part, has no idea that a sales offer is under way on that account. The minutes of the meeting where that customer was discussed sit in a video tool, never linked to the customer record.

Each tool holds a fragment of the truth, but nobody has the full picture. The customer notices immediately: they have to repeat the same information to three different people at the same company. That is not a detail of internal organisation — it is what shapes their image of you.

Hidden cost no. 2: time lost passing information around by hand

When tools do not communicate, people become the bridge between them. Copying information from an Excel file into an email. Forwarding a WhatsApp message to someone who was not in the conversation. Writing up what was said in a meeting, because the minutes exist nowhere except in the memory of whoever was there.

Taken individually, each of these tasks takes two minutes. Multiplied by the number of customers, technicians and weeks in a year, it adds up to a volume of invisible admin work — never budgeted, never visible on an invoice, but very real. It is time that nobody bills for and that adds no value: its only purpose is to make up for the fact that the tools do not talk to each other.

Hidden cost no. 3: no overall picture, and the risk of things being forgotten

Without connected data, it is hard to give a simple answer to questions that are actually basic: what is the real annual revenue from this customer? How many jobs have we done for them? Did anyone follow up after their last call? Each answer means cross-checking three or four sources by hand, hoping none has been overlooked along the way.

This lack of clarity has a direct cost: follow-ups that never happen, quotes that go nowhere, unhappy customers discovered too late because the warning got stuck in a WhatsApp thread nobody reread. The problem is never the price of a single tool. It is the number of times information has to change hands without ever changing tools.

The honest calculation: separate tools vs a connected suite

Let us do the maths without dressing it up. At Indusiax, if you subscribe to the four tools separately, per user per year, you pay: €650 for Stator-CRM, €390 for Rotor-FSM, €120 for Vector-MOM, €120 for Flux-Link. Total: €1,280 per user per year.

The full suite, which connects the four tools — single sign-on, customer records shared between Stator and Rotor, Vector meeting minutes fed automatically into Stator — costs €990 per user per year.

The difference is €290 per user per year, a saving of around 23% compared with the four tools taken separately. It is neither negligible nor miraculous — it is a real saving, but it is not the main argument. The main argument is that these €29 come with a benefit no price table shows: data that flows on its own, without anyone having to copy it out.

The suite is not a mandatory starting point

Let us be clear on one point: you do not need to start with all four tools connected. Most industrial SMEs that join us start with one specific need — often the CRM, sometimes field service management — and then add the other tools as the need is confirmed.

Start small, grow later

Vector-MOM and Flux-Link are subscribed to monthly, with no commitment. Stator-CRM and Rotor-FSM, which are for business customers only, are taken annually, on quotation. All have a 30-day free trial, no credit card required. You can test Rotor-FSM on its own for a few months, see whether the need for a CRM is confirmed, then switch to the full suite when the connection between the tools becomes a real gain rather than a theoretical convenience. When connecting the tools starts to pay off

In practice, switching to the full suite makes sense as soon as you use at least two or three of these tools in parallel and find that information already has to move between them — even manually. If the sales rep and the technician work on the same customers, if meeting minutes mention accounts already tracked in the CRM, the connection stops being a luxury: it replaces work that someone is already doing by hand, every week.

The same reasoning applied to a technician's time rather than to subscriptions: what an hour of a field technician's time really costs.

Do you have to take the whole Indusiax Suite from the start?

No. You can subscribe to a single tool — Stator-CRM, Rotor-FSM, Vector-MOM or Flux-Link — and add the others later, once the need is confirmed.

Can you start with one tool and add the others later?

Yes, in fact that is the most common path. Vector-MOM and Flux-Link are subscribed to monthly; Stator-CRM and Rotor-FSM, which are for business customers only, involve a twelve-month commitment. That lets you adjust step by step.

How much does the full suite cost?

€990 per user per year for the four connected tools, compared with €1,280 if you subscribe to them separately — a saving of around 23%, not counting the time saved by having connected data.

What happens if I only use one tool?

Nothing restrictive: the tool works on its own, at the price stated for that tool alone. The connection between tools is only activated if you move to the suite.

Software sprawl does not show up on an invoice — it shows up in lost time and missed opportunities. Whether you stick with a single tool or move to the full suite, the real question is not "how much does it cost?" but "how many times this week did I have to copy information from one tool into another?"
