Field service management software: moving beyond the notebook and the spreadsheet

Illegible notebooks, out-of-date spreadsheets, reports re-keyed in the evening: how dedicated field software solves these problems, one by one.

Indusiax20 July 20268 min read

A paper notebook lying at the back of a van. A shared Excel file that nobody opens in its latest version. A customer report typed up in the evening, from memory, because there was no time on site. If this sounds familiar, you are not alone — it is the daily reality for most companies that manage field service jobs: maintenance, inspection, after-sales service, technical assessments.

The answer fits in one sentence: field service management software replaces paper and spreadsheets with a single chain — scheduling, on-site inspection form, PDF report, machine history — accessible to everyone, always up to date, with no re-keying.

What remains is to understand why the notebook and Excel always end up breaking down, and what a tool designed for the field actually changes, round after round.

The notebook and the Excel file: a system that holds until the first surprise

The paper notebook has one merit: it is simple. But it also has three flaws that always end up costing dearly. It can be lost or left behind in a vehicle. It becomes illegible after a day in the rain or scribbled in between two jobs. And above all, there is only one copy: if the technician is on leave or unavailable, nobody else has the information.

The shared Excel file seems to solve the duplication problem — but creates another one. Every technician has to remember to update it, often in the evening, back at the office. The result: the schedule on screen is never quite the one in the field. Two technicians turn up at the same site. A customer appointment is forgotten because it was never written down anywhere. And when a file is edited by two people at the same time, the most recent version overwrites the other — sometimes wiping out a confirmed appointment.

On top of that comes a more insidious problem: re-keying. The technician notes observations in the field, then types them up again in the evening in a Word report for the customer. That re-keying time is not billed, it lengthens the working day, and it introduces errors — a dimension copied out wrongly, a forgotten photo, a detail that existed only in a memory that was already fading.

A schedule everyone can see, in real time

The first change a dedicated tool brings is the end of the schedule that lives in one person's head. Every job — new, rescheduled, cancelled — is visible immediately to the whole team, on a computer as well as on a tablet. No more calling the office to find out who is where: the field schedule is the same for everyone, continuously up to date.

The inspection form completed on site, photos included

This is the heart of the change. Rather than jotting observations in a notebook to write them up later, the technician fills in the inspection form directly on a tablet, during the job — with photos taken on the spot and automatically attached to the right machine and the right inspection point. Nothing to retype, nothing to reconstruct from memory back at base.

The time saved is not just the evening re-keying — it is also the reliability of a report written in the moment, not reconstructed from memory several hours later.

This is precisely the approach behind Rotor-FSM, the Indusiax tool dedicated to managing field service jobs and machine inspections: scheduling, inspection form, photos and report are brought together in a single flow, from the first appointment to handing the document to the customer.

The professional PDF report, generated straight away

Once the inspection form is complete, the PDF report is generated automatically — professional layout, company logo, embedded photos, observations sorted. No more laying out reports by hand in Word, one after another. The document can be handed to the customer quickly, which reinforces the company's image of professionalism and avoids back-and-forth emails to resend a forgotten or corrected report.

For a maintenance or after-sales service company, this detail changes how the customer sees you: a clean report that is consistent from one job to the next counts as much as a job well done.

Machine-by-machine history, finally accessible

With a notebook or a spreadsheet, finding a machine's complete history — past jobs, faults already recorded, how wear has progressed — often means digging through folders or relying on the memory of whichever technician was there last time. Dedicated software keeps that history for each machine, accessible in a few seconds before a new job. You know what has already been reported, what has been repaired and what still needs watching — without depending on the person who was on site the time before.

Regulatory inspections (VGP) and inspection bodies: traceability is not negotiable

For providers of regulatory inspections — vérifications générales périodiques (VGP — France's mandatory periodic inspections), inspection bodies, accredited organisations — field work comes with an extra constraint: the report handed to the customer is not just an account of the visit, it is a compliance document. It must be precise, dated and complete, and it must be retrievable years later in the event of an audit or a dispute.

Structured VGP software avoids two classic risks of the paper notebook: missing an inspection point on the form, and losing the original document altogether. Every inspection follows a consistent template, every photo is time-stamped and attached to the right machine, and every report stays accessible in the history — including to quickly retrieve an earlier inspection at the request of a customer or an inspector. That is also what distinguishes a properly conducted field inspection from a mere visit: the record it leaves behind.
